# Drum Hero

Drum Hero is a focused drum-learning studio for the browser. It combines a curated beginner-to-advanced curriculum with a playable timing trainer, rudiments, grooves, kit guidance, and private local progress.

## Highlights

- Twelve guided lessons across beginner, intermediate, and advanced levels
- Guided lesson notes with saved practice checkpoints and direct exercise handoffs
- Search launcher for pages, lessons, grooves, rudiments, and saved songs (`Ctrl/⌘ K`), plus a clickable curriculum skill map and silent technique demonstrations
- Five-voice keyboard and touch kit: Kick (`Space`), Snare (`F`), Hi-hat (`J`), Tom (`K`), Crash (`L`)
- Four-count, 40–200 BPM playback, pause/resume/reset, and visible subdivision playhead
- Deterministic Great/Good/Miss/Extra scoring with early/late offsets and combo tracking
- Local recorded drum samples and metronome cues
- Versioned browser-local progress; optional private per-learner sync for Practice Pad, Trainer, and Rhythm ear training, plus separate Song Library sharing; microphone permission is optional for Trainer audio input
- Responsive, reduced-motion-aware interface with keyboard and screen-reader support
- Reusable lazy-loaded Three.js drum-kit engine with procedural five-voice kit, articulated hands/sticks, pointer picking, and deterministic hit animation state
- Adjustable 4/8/10/16-repetition Trainer rounds with configurable count-in, self ratings, progressive tempo, and scored keyboard, touch, USB MIDI, or local audio input
- Notation-first Trainer practice with bar-by-bar screen-reader text, print/PDF and MusicXML score export, and MIDI pattern exchange
- A local, pattern-revision-aware Trainer editorial queue and anonymous opt-in player pilot notes with a multi-export summary command; neither authenticates reviewer roles or replaces real-player validation
- A private two-device Trainer duet for synchronized scored call-and-response and local sample playback from note events; microphone audio is not transmitted. Apply the Trainer Realtime migration before using it with Supabase.
- Custom practice routines with per-pattern tempo, repetitions, optional groove-to-fill transitions, and gated accuracy/timing checkpoints; routines travel in setup exports
- A 75-pattern groove library from first backbeats to shuffle, funk, Latin, compound and odd meters, plus groove-to-fill studies
- A local custom groove editor with eighth- and sixteenth-note grids, accents, and direct Trainer and Practice Pad handoff
- A generated C–G bass pulse for 4/4 Practice Pad patterns; Trainer also offers swing, laid-back timing, and sparse click modes
- Full local backup and restore for progress, Trainer settings, songs, custom grooves, imported samples, and recorded song takes
- Home first-session paths for a guided kit tour, touch/keyboard practice, or electronic kit
- A Home review queue that promotes due Song Library sections for the active learner, lesson skills without follow-up practice, and consistently weak reliable Trainer patterns; it moves the highest-priority review into the next-action card
- Web, iOS, and Android learning archives carry lessons, practice summaries, Trainer summaries, optional Trainer hit timing and ear-training history, guided sessions, weekly goals, songs, and grooves; Android preserves fields it does not interpret for later round-trip export
- Shared-device learner profiles with separate lesson progress, Trainer and Rhythm ear-training history, challenge packs, and Song Library practice history, notes, and recordings; song charts and custom grooves remain shared
- A backup-panel data-scope summary explains active-profile Song Library data alongside shared charts and device-wide backing audio
- Classroom overview of assignment completion, overdue work, recent accuracy, and last practice, with permission-based learner progress report export/import across devices
- Optional private cloud sync for learner progress, Trainer history, and Rhythm ear-training history, review schedules, and challenge packs, with conflict review, revision recovery, export, and deletion controls; teacher reports remain file transfers
- Downloadable offline shells for the learning and practice pages, built-in drum sounds, update notices, and storage/integrity controls; updates stage in a separate cache and preserve the last complete download until the new copy is complete
- Original synthesized rock, funk, and shuffle loops plus local user-provided backing audio in Play-alongs and Song Builder, with BPM/downbeat alignment and sync; editable forms, tempo maps, section fills, and mix controls
- Seated/no-kick and one-voice Trainer adaptations on web and iOS, excluded from full-pattern mastery comparisons, with a locally reviewable, exportable, and deletable player/educator feedback log
- Device timing repeatability log with review-before-export and deletion controls for comparing three calibrations on the same setup
- Consent-gated timing diagnostics export with browser capabilities and aggregate scores only; it excludes audio, device IDs, hit-by-hit data, and personal notes
- Vitest unit coverage plus Playwright functional, accessibility, responsive, and visual QA

Newer page introductions, curriculum map labels, launcher controls, technique-demo controls, and selected audio, feedback, and diagnostic copy have reviewed translations for English, Spanish, French, German, and Brazilian Portuguese. Other supported locales currently fall back to the English source for newer copy; deeper interactive Trainer and library copy still needs a full locale review.

## 3D kit engine

`lib/drum-engine.ts` owns the renderer, scene, camera, metre-scale `professional-v2` procedural drumset, kick pedal/beater, hands, sticks, hit mapping, picking, resize handling, and disposal. `lib/drum-dimensions.ts` keeps the conventional 22-inch kick, 14-inch snare, 10/12-inch rack toms, 16-inch floor tom, 14/16/18/20-inch cymbals, 5A stick, and adult hand reference dimensions in one immutable module. `lib/drum-animation.ts` keeps the bounded contact-and-rebound math framework-independent for deterministic unit tests and future trainers. `components/DrumKitCanvas.tsx` imports Three.js only on the client, exposes an imperative `hit(instrument, hand?, velocity?)` handle, supports `view="player"` and `view="showcase"` presets, adds focused-canvas keyboard controls on the Kit Guide, and provides a semantic fallback when WebGL is unavailable. Practice Pad sends accepted hits to the engine alongside its existing scoring/audio path; Kit Guide mounts the larger interactive viewer.

The renderer caps device pixel ratio at 1.5, avoids shadows/post-processing, renders only while hit animation is active, pauses naturally when idle, and disposes listeners, observers, materials, geometry, and renderer on unmount. Reduced-motion preferences shorten the visual response while preserving timing and accessibility.

## Rhythm ear training

Open `/ear-training` from the Practice menu or search launcher. Tap a heard rhythm back with the drum pads, keyboard, touch, or an optional microphone, or choose its matching rhythm grid. Separate rounds ask you to name the meter or recognize straight, swing, shuffle, pushed, and laid-back feel. Practice in 4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/4, 5/8, grouped 7/8, 9/8, 11/8, 12/8, and 13/8; choose one- or two-bar phrases and a 0–2 bar count-in; and set tempo from 40–140 BPM. Exercises cover eighth-note, sixteenth-note, triplet, and multi-voice grooves, with open, rock, funk, and clave families.

Choose guided, faded, or one-listen recall challenges, then add an optional three- or five-second memory delay. Adaptive progression changes one of rhythm density, phrase length, or tempo at a time. Dictation lets you transcribe a heard multi-voice pattern directly on the drum staff by choosing a drum voice and note subdivision; the step grid remains an optional fallback. Results, rhythm-builder comparisons, and identify choices put drum-staff notation first, with missed and extra hits and early or late timing marked on the score. Tuplet and cross-rhythm lessons add grouping labels to the notation. Notation support can stay visible through the answer or fade to count-only notation after listening; these supported attempts stay separate from ear-only scores. A miss can launch a focused one-bar review of that drum voice, kept out of first-attempt averages. Error detection compares repeated phrases; voice-isolation rounds ask you to echo one part of a full groove. New drills teach alternate 5/8, 7/8, 9/8, 11/8, and 13/8 groupings, pushed or laid-back snare placement, identifying which voice shifts between full grooves, and echoing a phrase at a different tempo. Tap back with pads, a microphone for single-drum rhythms, or a Web MIDI drum controller using General MIDI note mappings. Calibrate pads, microphone, and selected MIDI devices with an eight-click timing check; saved local offsets correct timing scores for that input. The click-dropout challenge supports all available meters with one to three silent bars. Thirteen guided lessons cover backbeat, half-time, shuffle, clave, compound 6/8, grouped 7/8, offbeat rests, a 3:2 cross-rhythm, a five-over-four quintuplet, compound 9/8 and 12/8, and 4:3 and 5:3 cross-rhythms, unlocking in sequence after two unassisted scores of 80% or higher. Personal challenge packs save up to 12 rhythms each, preview imported JSON before accepting it, and can be exported to share outside the app. Import a local audio clip to detect likely onsets, correct the rhythm on drum-staff notation, and save the resulting pattern to a pack; the clip is analyzed in-browser and is not uploaded. The rhythm builder authors kick, snare, and hi-hat parts with accents and ghost-note dynamics, plays them back, and can save them into a challenge pack. Results let learners replay the target and their response at slower or original speed; recent progress breaks down by meter, feel, subdivision, drum voice, and early/late timing. Unassisted results schedule weaker skills sooner for review. Custom 3-, 5-, or 10-drill sessions can focus on a selected skill, while a seven-day score chart and per-skill comparison of the latest three attempts against the previous three show recent progress. A stable weekly challenge adds a new local practice goal each Monday. Assisted attempts stay out of first-attempt averages. Results and skill progress remain separate from Trainer mastery and are included in full local backups; challenge packs are profile-specific, included in optional private learner progress sync, and exported separately. Input timing profiles stay in local browser storage. Ear training is included in newly downloaded offline page shells.

Tap-back rounds can score MIDI velocity, touch pressure, or microphone strike strength against accent and ghost-note targets, and the diagnostics view adds beat-and-subdivision timing detail. Results can replay either bar of a two-bar phrase. MIDI note mappings are configurable per controller, and any current rhythm can be exported as a MIDI file. A MIDI import flow maps pitches to drum voices, lets learners select a one- or two-bar passage, and shows notation before saving it to a challenge pack. A tempo-endurance challenge tracks pulse accuracy through a gradual eight-bar tempo ramp. Teachers can assign curated ear-training lessons through the existing class roster and track unassisted evidence, or export an assignment JSON with a learner and due date; learner reports carry scored error locations, and the teacher view aggregates common errors on the assigned drum notation. This file-based exchange keeps assignments and reports portable without requiring accounts or a server.

## Trainer inputs

Open `/trainer`, choose from 100 groove and fill patterns, a rudiment, a saved custom groove, or a song part. Fill practice can play a groove for one, two, or four bars before a compatible fill; choose alternating, paradiddle, or double-stroke sticking and either lead hand. Loop a beat or bar range for focused repetition. The groove library can save reusable groove-to-fill transitions. Trainer step labels show beat and subdivision counts, with optional browser speech for beat or grid counts. Choose 4, 8, 10, or 16 repetitions and a one-, two-, or four-beat count-in. Self-rated rounds pause after each pass for Clean, Needs work, or Missed; scored recaps report accuracy, timing steadiness, hit timing across the phrase, and misses by drum voice. Choose straight, swung, or laid-back timing targets, sparse metronome clicks, and hands-only or feet-only practice. Flam, drag, and buzz rudiments show articulation cues and score their grace strokes. Personal routines save on this device, set tempo and repetitions per pattern, add groove-to-fill transitions, and optionally gate each step on accuracy and timing goals; setup exports carry the routine and any custom or song-part patterns it uses. The tempo ladder lets you set its pass target, step size, and response to a missed target. Guided paths gate each pattern on configurable accuracy and timing goals, while adaptive plans prioritize due review patterns and weak spots. Microphone dynamics can be calibrated per drum voice for ghost taps and accents; input diagnostics show recent peak level and warn when the signal clips. Recording review overlays expected and recorded hit markers on the latest waveform, and saved reference takes support A/B comparison. Export and import Trainer setups as JSON. The progress view plots recent scored accuracy and summarizes timing by drum voice.

Keyboard and touch work without device permission. USB MIDI uses browser Web MIDI, per-device note maps, and velocity feedback. A short click-and-tap wizard measures input timing for each device. The Trainer summarizes browser capabilities, saved correction, and recent timing for the selected input profile; results are not a universal hardware benchmark. Audio input uses microphone or line-in permission and stays on the device. Trainer voice scoring calibrates five core kit voices; Song practice calibrates all eight Song Library voices from five strikes per voice and keeps separate profiles for each saved kit type and tuning, cymbal, and pedal setup. Both leave uncertain hits unscored and keep calibration data locally. Optional round recordings stay in page memory and are not uploaded; audio used for scoring is not saved. See the [device validation plan](docs/DEVICE_VALIDATION.md).

## Scoring

- **Great:** within 50 ms of the expected hit
- **Good:** within 100 ms
- **Miss:** more than 100 ms late or never played
- **Extra:** no matching target within 100 ms

The score averages 100 points for Great and 70 for Good across expected hits, then deducts two points per extra hit. Hardware, browser, display, and audio latency can influence perceived timing.

## Privacy and safety

Drum Hero stores settings, lesson completion, custom grooves, and up to 50 recent Practice Pad and Trainer sessions in `localStorage`. It requests microphone or line-in access only when you connect Trainer audio input and does not upload Trainer input. Optional account sync uploads Song Library data, the active learner's progress, or a teacher assignment roster only after you connect and enable it. Opt-in classroom groups share account membership, class assignments, and learner-submitted completion notes; they do not share practice scores, history, profiles, audio, or recordings. See the [classroom data policy](docs/CLASSROOM_DATA_POLICY.md) and apply its migration before production use. Progress snapshots contain lesson completion, scores, Trainer rounds, and MIDI/latency calibration settings; Song practice microphone voice templates, song recordings, and reference audio remain local. Shared-device profiles and assignments remain local by default. A learner or guardian can explicitly export a compact progress report for a teacher to import on another device; it includes lesson completions and scored practice summaries, but no audio or recordings. Imported reports stay on the receiving device and can be removed from the classroom overview. Adaptive feedback and device-validation logs also stay local unless explicitly exported. Audio input includes room-noise measurement to help set a detection threshold. Song Library practice history, recordings, reference audio, and notes follow the active learner profile; charts remain shared on this device. Cross-platform song transfer packs contain charts, embedded arrangement notes, and source attribution, but exclude learner goals, practice history, journals, annotations, recordings, and reference audio. iOS can opt in to append-only song exchange with an editable account library; conflict revisions are reloaded for review, and unsupported mobile voices are skipped. Original MusicXML stays local. Public repertoire submissions are separately rights-attested, sanitized, and held for independent review before publication. Song Library recordings are saved locally only after you choose Record, and can be exported or deleted. Acoustic drums can damage hearing; use suitable hearing protection and stop if playing causes pain or numbness.

### Rehearsal, sharing, and approved arrangements

In Trainer, **Timed rehearsal with rests** plays the patterns in your personal routine for 30, 60, or 120 seconds each, followed by a selectable rest and a next-pattern preview. **Private peer challenges** exports the selected pattern, tempo, goal, target accuracy, duration, and attribution as JSON. The file excludes profile data, scores, recordings, and audio IDs. **Manage local audio storage**, inside Trainer's saved recordings and the Progress backup panel, shows original song/Trainer audio sizes, exports individual originals, and deletes selected recordings after confirmation. ### Sight reading

Open **Practice → Sight reading** (or search for it with Ctrl/⌘ K). Choose a level, 40–200 quarter-note BPM, and one, two, or four bars. Start reveals a fresh staff for a ten-second silent preview, then a one-bar count-in and one scored pass. Beginner phrases alternate quarter/eighth-note reading; intermediate adds sixteenths and 3/4; advanced adds five voices, 5/4, 7/8 grouped 2+2+3, and triplets in quarter-note meters. Accents are reading cues and do not affect timing/voice scores.

Web supports keyboard, touch pads, and Web MIDI where available. The iOS screen is available from Learn and Trainer and supports touch, external keyboard, and Core MIDI. Mapping and timing corrections reuse Trainer settings. Microphone scoring is outside this mode. Native currently has one learner; web history follows the selected learner profile.

Only the first uninterrupted attempt enters reading trends. Retrying the same exercise, including after cancellation, is practice; optional spoken notes on web mark an attempt assisted. Leaving the page, backgrounding, or losing the selected MIDI input ends the attempt. Completed attempts remain separate from Trainer mastery, with the latest 100 saved locally and included in full backups. Reference playback becomes available after completion. Older native backups without reading history preserve the current history. Neither platform uploads reading results.
