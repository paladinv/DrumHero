# Initial assessment educator pilot

The assessment is a product starting-point estimate. Its thresholds and content have not been validated as standardized placement norms. Use this guide to gather educator review before making stronger placement claims.

## Review workflow

1. Invite drum teachers to complete the assessment with learners who represent the intended beginner and returning-player audiences. Record input type and adaptation separately; keyboard and touch coordination does not establish physical limb independence.
2. Ask the teacher to estimate pulse, subdivision, reading, and coordination skill independently before showing the app result. Compare the teacher's estimate with the demonstrated tiers and the recommended lesson.
3. Use **Download anonymous educator review** on web or **Share anonymous educator review** on iOS to export the task-level evidence. The JSON includes the assessment content version, item prompts and answers, probe variants, hit match percentage, score, misses, extras, timing bias, timing spread, and dynamics contrast. It excludes the learner name, result identifier, date, and MIDI device ID. Sharing is initiated by the learner and stays under their control.
4. For repeatability, retake with the same input and adaptation. A failed tier uses its equivalent alternate pattern; compare tier placement and diagnostic evidence, and note whether device calibration changed.
5. Review listening and notation items for ambiguous wording, alternate defensible answers, and notation that is difficult to access with screen readers. Keep any revised question or probe in the versioned shared content and rerun the TypeScript and Swift fixtures.

## Review notes to capture

- Learner experience level and teacher's independent starting-point estimate by skill.
- Input category, adaptation, selected audio route, and whether the device was calibrated.
- Whether directions and demonstrations were understood without coaching.
- Whether the displayed tier and lesson recommendation fit the teacher's estimate; note the evidence that supports a disagreement.
- Whether the alternate retry felt equivalent in difficulty and tested the same skill.
- Any accessibility issue, ambiguous question, or probe that measured the input device more than the learner.

Keep comparisons separate by input and adaptation. Do not combine adapted and full-kit results into one norm, or interpret the current cutoffs as population percentiles. Educator review and a suitably designed validation study are required before the assessment is described as a certified or standardized placement measure.
